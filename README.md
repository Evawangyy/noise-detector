First I try the most sample one. When clapping or caughing, light is turned on and buzzer sounds.
During connecting wires, I cannot find P1.0 on the board.  Later I learned the silkscreen differs from common convention: to save space, boards often omit the dot and label P1.0 as P10.
Then I adjust the potentiometer. The goal is to turn the potentiometer in a quiet environment until the signal indicator LED just turns off. I turn the cross-head potentiometer counterclockwise with a small screwdriver. However, even after 15 to 20 turns, the switch indicator LED remain on. This make me realize that the threshold adjustment might be in the opposite direction. I then rotate it the other way and successfully turned the LED off.
Next I edite the code in Keil uVision4 and use the STC-ISP software to download it. It is important to note that you must turn the power switch of the development board off and then on again for the download to be successful. The code come from an online video tutorial.
After completing all this, I encounter a problem: the buzzer keep ringing as soon as I turn on the power. I unplug the DO pin from the KY-037 and keep turning the potentiometer with a screwdriver until the indicator LED just turn off. However, the buzzer still rang when I plug it back in. By asking AI, I find that the LM393 modules from manufacturers might reverse output logic compared to the standard: they output 0 (low level) when quiet and 1 (high level) when there is sound. For this type of module, adjusting until the LED turns off means it is outputting 0, and the microcontroller will trigger the alarm when it reads 0. Therefore, I modifie the code to if (SoundSensor == 1) {, and it worked. This way, the sound detection device starte working normally.
